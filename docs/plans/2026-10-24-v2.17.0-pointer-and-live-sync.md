# v2.17.0 plan: pointer interaction and live quota sync

**Goal:** Make the dashboard usable with the mouse alone (hover, click, wheel, drag) and keep the quota on screen close to real time, without asking Google much more often than today.

**Issues:** [#74](https://github.com/aklkbqx/agy-swap/issues/74) (scroll, rule, page air), [#75](https://github.com/aklkbqx/agy-swap/issues/75) (pointer), [#76](https://github.com/aklkbqx/agy-swap/issues/76) (live sync).

**Scope decided by the owner (2026-10-24):** everything below ships together as v2.17.0, with the scroll views, the movable stacked rule, the page air fixes and the removal of the quota overview page already on the branch (#74).

**Tech stack:** Go standard library and `golang.org/x/sys` only. No new dependencies.

## Part A: pointer interaction

### Findings

| Finding | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| The TUI asks for button and drag reports only | `enterScreen` sends `?1000h ?1002h ?1006h`; pointer moves without a button are never reported |
| Mouse targets are kept per feature | `listHits`/`listFirstRow` for list clicks, `wideSplitBounds` and `onStackRule` for the divider and rule, `scrollAreas` for the wheel |
| Dialogs, the palette, forms and key hints ignore the mouse | the mouse branch runs only in `tuiBrowse` mode on the dashboard |

### Design

- **One hit map.** Each frame records its targets once: `state.hits []tuiHit{kind, key, top, bottom, left, right}` in body rows and frame columns. Kinds: `account`, `divider`, `rule`, `scroll`, `chip` (a footer key hint), `button` (a dialog button), `palette` (a palette row), `card` (a pool card). The list, divider, rule and scroll-area code moves onto it, so clicks, the wheel and hover all ask the same question: what is under the pointer.
- **Hover.** Turn on any-motion reports (`?1003h`) and read button 35 as `mouse:move:col:row`. The reader coalesces a burst of moves into the latest one, and the loop repaints only when the target under the pointer changes. Hover never changes the selection; it only shows what a click would do:
  - an account row gets a faint tint (between the body and the selection gray);
  - the divider or rule lights in the dim action colour, saying it can be dragged;
  - a footer key chip and a dialog button light like a pressed key;
  - a palette row gets the palette's selection tint.
- **Click.** An account selects, a second click switches (both layouts). A key chip runs its key (`Enter Switch` asks to switch, `^K Actions` opens the palette). A dialog button presses it; a click outside a dialog cancels it. A palette row runs its action. A click on the scroll track pages the view; the thumb drags.
- **Pointer shape.** Where the terminal supports OSC 22 (kitty, foot, Ghostty, WezTerm), the pointer becomes a hand over clickable targets and a resize arrow over the divider and rule; other terminals ignore it.
- **Keyboard parity.** Everything stays reachable by keys; `AGY_SWAP_MOUSE=0` turns mouse reporting off for terminals or multiplexers that mishandle it.

## Part B: live quota sync

### Findings

| Finding | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Every account refreshes on one 60-second timer | `tuiAutoRefresh` drives `startRefresh(false, true)` |
| A due account is often skipped for a whole round | the timer fires when a snapshot is 59.x seconds old, and `refreshSelected` skips snapshots younger than `quotaCache` (60 s), so the screen can be about two minutes old |
| A reset time passing is noticed only on the next round | nothing is scheduled at a pool's `reset_at` |
| Each refresh makes two or three Google calls per account | `loadCodeAssist` then `retrieveUserQuotaSummary` (plus `fetchAvailableModels` when Gemini is missing) |
| Log limits are read only during a refresh | `Store` reconciles `LogScanner.Scan()` results when accounts load |

### Design

- **Per-account schedule.** A pure function decides when each account is next due: the active account every 20 s, the others every 2 min, staggered; an account whose shared pools are used up waits for its earliest reset instead. Snapshots count as due a little early (a 5 s slack), so a timer tick never skips them.
- **Refresh at reset.** A timer fires a few seconds after the earliest `reset_at` on screen and refreshes that account.
- **Countdowns tick.** The screen repaints when the minute changes, so "resets in …" never stands still.
- **Fewer calls.** The Code Assist project and tier are remembered per account for 15 minutes in memory; any error drops them and the next refresh takes the full path again, so eligibility (`VERIFY`) is rechecked at least that often.
- **Back-off.** A 429 or 5xx doubles that account's interval (with jitter, up to 10 minutes) and a success resets it.
- **Follow Antigravity.** Every 2 s the TUI checks the modification times of Antigravity's log and session files (no reads); fresh activity refreshes the active account within about 5 s, and a rate-limit line in a new log segment marks the cooldown at once.
- **Other agy-swap processes.** A change to `accounts.json` from another process (`agy-swap next` in another window, `watch`, the statusline) reloads the TUI.
- **Clock skew.** The `Date` header of Google's replies corrects reset countdowns on a machine whose clock is off.

### Acceptance

- The active account's numbers are at most about 20 s old while online; a passed reset shows full within about 5 s; usage in Antigravity shows within about 5–10 s.
- Google calls per hour stay within 1.5 times today's for the same accounts, measured before and after with `AGY_SWAP_QUOTA_DEBUG=1`.

## Calls to Google per hour

Counted per account, before and after (a read was two requests; now the project lookup is remembered for 15 minutes, so a read is one request plus four lookups an hour):

| | Before (intended: every 60 s) | Before (observed: every ~120 s, see the skipped round) | After |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Account in use, Antigravity idle | 120 | 60 | 184 (every 20 s) |
| Account in use, Antigravity busy | 120 | 60 | at most 364 (one extra read per 10 s at most) |
| Each other account | 120 | 60 | 34 (every 2 min) |
| Four accounts, idle | 480 | 240 | 286 |

With four accounts and Antigravity idle the TUI asks about 1.2 times as often as it did in practice and 0.6 times as often as it was meant to; `TestARepeatReadSkipsTheProjectLookup` counts the requests.
